# UGN Campus Bible — Reusable Template (new)

*An author-facing setting architecture for the University of Greater Neptune's four campuses and Varuna Station*

Version 1.0 · November 2026

**Use:** Make a separate Bible for Neptune, Cadence, Middle Fork, Amity Gate, and the Station. This is a purpose-built remake drawing on Serena's *Fictional Setting Description*, *Fictional Town Documentation*, *Location Lore*, and *Roads and Routes* templates. The source templates remain unchanged. Complete every section with specific observed details, artifacts, habits, boundaries, and scene tests. Established regional canon outranks the prompts.

## 1. The Campus in One Glance
- **Formal name, local names, founding and institutional lineage:** What would a diploma say, and what would a bus passenger say?
- **Place on the map:** City, neighborhood, access point, region, road/rail/water relationships; cite the Road Atlas rather than invent travel times.
- **Students, primary disciplines, sports identity:** Specific numbers or a sensible range; distinguish systemwide enrollment.
- **What the university says it is / what the neighbors say:** Give two competing accurate descriptions.

## 2. The Ground Before the School
- **Previous uses:** Whose labor, ownership, communities, or environments shaped this ground?
- **Founding moment:** What need caused a school to open here, and who could actually attend?
- **Dated changes:** Campus name changes, mergers, expansion, disasters and fights.
- **The record kept and the record neglected:** A plaque, an archive, an oral memory, a disputed site.

## 3. The Campus Underfoot
- **Physical orientation:** Arrival edge, center, waterfront/road/forest edge, elevations, prevailing weather.
- **A reliable walking route:** Six to eight named stops in order and what is visible between them.
- **Where public land ends:** Working industry, neighborhoods, protected lands, sovereign lands, bridges, fences, service routes.
- **Access and limits:** Transit, walking, bicycles, car use, disability access, weather closures and evening routes.

## 4. Buildings That Have a Life
- **Oldest still-used structure:** Architecture, original purpose, present tenants, scars.
- **Teaching places:** Classrooms, labs, library, studios, clinical sites, workshops.
- **Living places:** Dorms if any, rental streets, study nooks, food, restrooms and after-hours refuge.
- **The overlooked room:** Maintenance, lost-property desk, back stair, coat rack, storage or service corridor that can carry a scene.
- **A building's contested future:** Repair, preservation, expansion or displacement.

## 5. The Academic Map
- **Signature schools:** Actual programs, their research tools and why they exist here.
- **Two ordinary course days:** One for a commuter and one for a resident, field student or working adult.
- **Placements and apprenticeships:** Partners outside campus, who gets paid, and how weather changes the work.
- **The campus's blind spots:** Disciplines it doesn't offer; where students go instead.

## 6. Who Can Afford to Be Here
- **Student makeup and pathways:** Local, first-generation, transfer, adult, off-region; no single default student.
- **Housing and money:** Rent, food, books, jobs, scholarships, tuition pressures and commuting.
- **Support and friction:** Advising, disability access, library hours, clinical or field requirements and bureaucratic obstacles.
- **Three distinct student realities:** Give practical weekly patterns with different stakes.

## 7. The People Who Keep It Running
- **Governance:** System-level board and campus-level authority; distinguish government bodies.
- **Labor:** Faculty, adjuncts, researchers, custodians, cooks, facilities, security, trades and student workers.
- **Neighbors and partners:** The town, port, hospital, schools, Nation, land agency or private employers, as applicable.
- **A plausible conflict of interests:** Who can say yes, who pays, who gets represented, who lives next door?

## 8. The Public Memory and the Claim
- **A celebrated event:** How the institution stages it.
- **An injury the city remembers:** Name affected people or communities from established canon; avoid invented consensus.
- **What accountability looks like:** Records, access, restitution requests, protective commitments or unresolved obligations.
- **Author's knowledge only:** Where an institutional statement and a resident's recollection disagree; no fabricated secret supernatural cause.

## 9. A Year of Campus Life
- **Autumn, winter, spring and summer:** One exact sound, surface, smell and practical change per season.
- **Time-of-day map:** Before dawn, class hours, dusk, after midnight.
- **Weather test:** How a missed ferry, closed road, high swell, smoke day or snow closure changes a real person's plans.

## 10. Rituals, Teams and Student Speech
- **Sports, mascot, colors and contested pride:** Existing canon first.
- **Traditions:** A formal event and an unofficial act, with consequences.
- **Local vocabulary:** Campus nicknames; never impose one campus's identity on another.
- **Three overheard lines:** Different people, different stakes; no exposition masquerading as dialogue.

## 11. Story Routes and Pressure Points
- **Three scene paths:** Give starts, obstacles, destinations, and an action at each.
- **Five reusable story situations:** Everyday stakes; usable across distinct books without importing their protagonists.
- **A mundane contradiction that will outlast a book:** A city/campus dispute without an automatic villain or easy resolution.

## 12. The Continuity Ledger
- **Established:** Facts already fixed in the shared Master Reference and campus/region companions.
- **New public-register development:** Detail adopted in this Bible; cross-reference exact source files.
- **Held:** Existing protected mysteries, with no hints or solutions.
- **Do not invent:** New supernatural rules, new road-mile figures, a new UGN claim over Lhunpasi College, or characters crossing separate novels.
- **Checks before reuse:** Historical date, geography, governing body, student count, campus nickname, weather, season, and whether a character could actually get from stop A to B.

*Template discipline: populate this Bible as prose and location cards rather than a questionnaire. Preserve the character-specific and Nation-specific boundaries of the larger universe. A future campus can use this same twelve-part spine while changing the details that define its daily life.*
