# LOW WATER — Half a Step

Neptune Public Radio · A fictional full-cast broadcast · 3:11 a.m.

**Cast:** Winnie Kask, Yoli Pruneda, Kino Benavides, June.

**Yoli Pruneda:** Winnie, I want you to listen to something. I'm at the flume in Sweetbriar. I've pulled over, engine off. Listen. Did you hear that? Did you hear it fall?

**Winnie Kask:** All right, Yoli. I'm hearing water and what sounds like a small engine. Tell me what I'm listening for.

**Yoli Pruneda:** That low hum behind me. That's the Sweetbriar flume. It always hums one note. Been listening to it since I was a kid. About five minutes ago, I swear to God, it dropped.

**Winnie Kask:** Dropped how? Went quiet? Slowed down? And while we're doing this, please tell me you're somewhere safe.

**Yoli Pruneda:** I'm in the wide gravel turnout by the canal gate. Truck's behind the rail, hazards going, vest on. Everybody's safe, Winnie. I'm talking about pitch. The note went lower. Half a step, maybe.

**Winnie Kask:** Okay. Half a step. Those of you who don't know Sweetbriar, the power canal runs right through the town. You can hear it from the grocery store if the wind's right.

**Yoli Pruneda:** And you know the story. When the flume drops half a step, somebody leaves for good. People tell it to scare teenagers, but my grandmother swore it happened the night her sister moved away.

**Winnie Kask:** I know the story. I also know you said you pulled over for a reason. Did you call to tell me about the noise, or is something happening out there?

**Kino Benavides:** We've got two texts. One says it's probably a pump switching over. The other says, quote, don't let Yoli decide what a musical half step is. That's from Merle.

**Winnie Kask:** Merle's driving a fuel truck over a mountain in the dark and apparently still has time to review the region's music theory. Yoli, you heard Kino. Is it a pump?

**Yoli Pruneda:** It could be. I'm not running the power company. But there's something else. A burgundy wagon's been parked up the shoulder more than an hour, with the driver's door open. Nobody else has come by it.

**Winnie Kask:** Have you gone over? From where you're sitting, can you see anybody? And I want you to stay off the road, please. The fog's thick on that stretch.

**Yoli Pruneda:** I can see someone in the front seat now. A girl, I think. She sat up when I turned the truck lights on. I waved. She waved back. I'd go over, but the shoulder is narrow.

**Kino Benavides:** Message from Sweetbriar. The flume changes pitch sometimes when they adjust the gates. A listener says her husband works nights on the canal and she'll text him. Another listener says he isn't there tonight.

**Winnie Kask:** That's useful, actually. We have a possible mechanical explanation, and a real person in a car who might need help. Yoli, keep your truck where it is. Kino, can you try the county nonemergency number?

**Yoli Pruneda:** I have a jump pack in the truck. If her battery's dead, I can get her going. I'm not going to run across traffic after it in this fog. There is a phone number painted on the canal gate, though.

**Winnie Kask:** All right. We can solve the car first. If the person in the wagon is listening, this is Low Water, and your neighbors are worried about you. You can call us. No one is in trouble.

**Kino Benavides:** Line three just lit up. Caller says she's in the burgundy wagon. Her name's June.

**Winnie Kask:** June, hello. I'm Winnie. You can hear me okay?

**June:** I can. Hi. Sorry. I heard the woman on the radio describing my car, and I thought I ought to say something before somebody calls the police. I'm fine. I'm just... I'm a little stuck.

**Winnie Kask:** Okay. That's a good place to begin. Is the car running? Do you need us to call a tow?

**June:** The battery's dead, I think. I left the hazard lights on when I stopped. I didn't realize how long I was sitting there. It was only supposed to be a minute. My phone was almost dead too.

**Winnie Kask:** Yoli's on the other line. She's in the truck you can probably see down the road. She's got a jump pack. If you're comfortable, she can come help when the road is clear.

**June:** Oh. Okay. Yeah, please. She's got a yellow truck? I can see the lights. I'm sorry to make everybody worry. I really didn't mean to make this a whole thing on the radio.

**Yoli Pruneda:** It's a tow truck, honey. I have made a living off people's bad nights for twenty years. Yours is pretty mild so far. I'm coming up your side when this logging rig goes through.

**Winnie Kask:** There we are. We have a plan. June, you said you thought you were only stopping for a minute. Where were you headed at three in the morning?

**June:** Eugene. I got a job there. I have a room lined up, and a woman from work is giving me the key tomorrow. Well, today, I guess. I packed the car after supper and I just... left.

**Winnie Kask:** Is this your first move away from Sweetbriar?

**June:** Yeah. I'm twenty-three. My parents live two streets from the school. I've slept in the same bedroom since I was six. Everybody kept saying I should be excited. I AM excited. That's the problem. I'm scared and excited together.

**Winnie Kask:** What made you pull over? If you don't mind me asking.

**June:** I got past the grocery store, past the old softball field, and I couldn't see the town anymore in the rearview. I wanted one more look. And it was foggy, and the flume sounded strange, so I pulled over.

**Winnie Kask:** You know about the flume story, then.

**June:** Everybody knows. I used to tell my little brother it was true. And I didn't believe it, I really didn't. But I got out and listened, and it sounded lower. Like the whole town had gone to a different key.

**Winnie Kask:** Have you called your people and told them you're safe?

**June:** I texted my dad before I started the car. He wrote back, drive slow, and then he sent me a picture of our dog asleep on the kitchen floor. I kept looking at the picture. And looking. And then the battery died.

**Kino Benavides:** We have an answer from the power company, sort of. Someone who identifies as a canal operator says the gates are running normally, and changes in flow can alter the audible pitch. No faults reported tonight.

**Winnie Kask:** That's a reasonable answer. June, do you want to tell me what the job is? Sometimes it helps to say the next thing out loud.

**June:** It's the inventory office at a hospital. Not exciting to most people. I like keeping track of things. I'm really good at it. They offered full time, and benefits. I bought my own dishes. They're in a box by the spare tire.

**Winnie Kask:** That sounds like quite a lot of work you've already done to get yourself there.

**June:** It took me six months. I'd come home from the interview and sit in the driveway because I couldn't stand going inside and hearing everybody ask how it went. They mean well. I just... I don't know how to be somebody somewhere else.

**Yoli Pruneda:** I've got eyes on you now, June. Stay in your seat. I'm on the gravel, coming around the back. Winnie, this battery is older than my niece. Give me a minute with it.

**Winnie Kask:** You heard her. While she works, tell me one thing you're looking forward to, June. Something small. It doesn't have to be profound.

**June:** The window. My new room has a window over a little yard, and the woman who rents it says there's a pear tree. I can put a chair there. I can sit and look at something I've never looked at before.

**Winnie Kask:** A chair by a pear tree. I'd say that's a perfectly respectable thing to look forward to. We have a text from your part of the world, by the way. Kino?

**Kino Benavides:** Someone says, quote, tell the young lady Sweetbriar will still be there when she wants to come back for Sunday dinner. And also the canal's been out of tune since 1998. End quote.

**Winnie Kask:** Those two thoughts came from the same person, I assume. June, you've got a whole town listening to you, so I should probably apologize for that.

**June:** It's all right. I think I know who sent it. My aunt talks like that. She'd add a recipe if the text would let her. It's weird. I'm feeling better, actually.

**Yoli Pruneda:** Okay. Lights on. June, try the ignition. No gas, just turn it. There it is. That's an engine. Leave it running a while. I'll follow you to the junction just to be sure.

**June:** Oh my gosh. Thank you. Really, thank you, both of you. I was sitting here thinking I'd made a mistake before I'd even got out of town.

**Winnie Kask:** You can take your time at the junction. Get something warm to eat when there's somewhere open. And drive only if you feel awake enough for it. How does the road look from the car?

**June:** Clearer than it did. The fog's still there, but I can see the signs now. I think... I think I can go. Thanks for staying on with me.

**Winnie Kask:** Of course. Be safe, June. We'll be listening if you need us. Tell your dad we've got a good report for him, if you want.

**Yoli Pruneda:** Winnie? I'm back at my truck. She's pulling out. She's got her lights on. I'm going to follow her just past the bend. Hang on. The flume.

**Winnie Kask:** What about it?

**Yoli Pruneda:** I'm standing right beside it again. Sounds like it always did. Same note as every other night.

**Winnie Kask:** Well. That's good, then. You go see that she makes the bend.

**Kino Benavides:** New text from Merle. He would like the record to show that a semitone is measurable and the flume has never been issued a tuning fork.

**Winnie Kask:** His objection is on the record. It's a little after three twenty. Roads are wet between Sweetbriar and the Waist, fog along the river, no new closures on the Causeway. We're still here. If you're out there driving, keep your lights low and your eyes up. Kino, take the next call.
